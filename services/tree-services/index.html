Local know-how

Trees in the Triangle

Wake County is full of tall loblolly pines, oaks, maples and sweetgums, and many homes were built right among them. Summer thunderstorms and the remnants of tropical storms regularly bring down limbs and whole trees across the area.

The best time to prune most hardwoods is late fall through winter when they are dormant: you can see the branch structure and the tree heals well in spring. Dead limbs and hazardous trees, though, should be dealt with as soon as you notice them.

FAQ

Tree Services FAQ

When is the best time to trim trees in North Carolina?

For most shade trees, late fall through late winter is ideal because the tree is dormant and the branch structure is easy to see. Dead, broken or hazardous limbs can and should be removed any time of year.

What is the difference between stump grinding and stump removal?

Stump grinding grinds the stump down below ground level and leaves wood chips behind. Full removal pulls out the stump and major roots, which takes more work but clears the area completely, for example before planting or building.

How do I know if a tree is dangerous?

Warning signs include a tree that has started leaning recently, cracked or heaving soil at the base, large dead limbs, mushrooms on the trunk or roots, and deep cracks in the trunk. If you see any of these near your home, have it looked at soon.
